Busy Is Not the Same as Profitable

You are booked, you are working, and the money at the end of the month does not match the effort. Or the calendar has gaps you cannot explain, the good staff keep leaving, and you are the only one who can run an event properly.

Most catering operators who are stuck are stuck on one of two things, and often both.

The numbers. Labor priced by guesswork, menus that lose money on the dishes that sell best, food cost nobody is tracking, and cash that arrives after the bills do.

The people. Every booking coming from someone who already knows you, no system for finding the next one, and a team you cannot grow past because hiring is a panic and training happens on the job.

Fixing one and not the other caps the business either way. A full calendar at the wrong margins is exhausting and pointless. Perfect margins on a half-empty calendar is a hobby.

Part One — The Catering Profit System

Five modules on the numbers: what each job actually costs you, and what to charge for it.

  • Labor Cost Control — exact staffing per event type, the hidden cost of travel, setup and breakdown, and a bench team you can call on.
  • Pricing That Actually Works — full-cost pricing, service tiers, and quoting without discounting your way into the job.
  • Food Cost & Menu Engineering — recipe costing, which dishes earn their place, and where the waste goes.
  • Delivery & Logistics — routing, load planning, equipment, and the event-day checklist.
  • Cash Flow & Profitability — deposits, payment terms, break-even, and a monthly dashboard that tells you the truth.

Part Two — The Catering People System

Five modules on the people: the clients you have to find and keep, and the team that delivers the work.

  • Lead Generation — where catering buyers actually are, positioning for corporate against private, and an outreach system you can run weekly.
  • Marketing — messaging, social that books gigs rather than collects likes, local presence, and reviews asked for on purpose.
  • Winning and Keeping Clients — the sales conversation, proposals that hold up, referrals by system, and the difficult situations that decide whether they rebook.
  • Hiring and Staffing — who to hire and when, a process that filters before it costs you a shift, onboarding, and letting someone go properly.
  • Team and Culture — what makes staff stay, accountability without standing over people, and leading on event day when nothing goes to plan.
